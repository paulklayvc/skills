# Founder risk cases

Real cases behind the six risk areas in the review. Use them to explain why a term matters, not as proof that a particular investor will behave the same way. Keep the distinction between what is established and what one side alleges; several of these disputes were never adjudicated. Summarized October 2026.

## 1. Conflicts of interest — Factory, Cognition, and Khosla Ventures (September–October 2026)

**Established (reported):** On 2026-09-30 Factory CEO Matan Grinberg said he had fired Chris Degnan from his role as a board adviser, alleging that Degnan shared confidential information with Cognition, Factory's biggest competitor. Degnan — a partner at Factory investor RPT Partners and, separately, a go-to-market adviser at Iconiq — announced the same day that he had joined Cognition as chief revenue officer. Khosla Ventures is invested in both Factory and Cognition; Vinod Khosla publicly called Factory "a struggling second tier competitor", while Khosla Ventures' Keith Rabois sided with Factory. ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/30/factory-ceo-just-accused-his-vc-board-advisor-of-spying-for-cognition/))

**Disputed:** Degnan says he was not fired but resigned when he told Grinberg he was taking the job. There is no public evidence that confidential information moved.

**For the review:** the conflict here was personal, not a portfolio overlap: the fund did not need to back Cognition for the information risk to arise. Check the fund's portfolio, and also the other board seats, observer seats, advisory roles, and operating jobs of the specific person who will sit on the board, observe, or advise. Require confidentiality undertakings from that person, disclosure of new roles, exclusion from competitive matters, and automatic termination if they join or advise a competitor.

## 2. Rights changed by a majority — Valar Atomics and Day One Ventures (August–October 2026)

**Reported:** Valar Atomics filed a declaratory-relief suit in Delaware in October 2026 over Day One Ventures' pro rata rights in its Sequoia-led Series B, according to Valar CEO Isaiah Taylor's post of 2026-10-09; [The Information](https://www.theinformation.com/briefings/valar-atomics-sues-investor-day-one-funding-round-allocation) reported the suit (paywalled). The court filing itself has not been checked. ([Isaiah Taylor's post](https://x.com/isaiah_p_taylor/status/2108679133467414855))

**Valar's account:** the investors' rights agreement had a "spring-back" clause restoring waived pro rata rights if any Major Investor received allocation; when Day One refused to waive, Major Investors holding a majority amended the agreement the day before closing to remove the spring-back for everyone.

**Day One's account:** Valar had agreed that Day One's participation rights could not be eliminated without its consent; four of six Major Investors participated without waiving; Day One also raises a secondary sale and information requests. ([Masha Bucher's post](https://x.com/mashadrokova/status/2108732979191390528))

**Status:** unresolved.

**For the review:** for each right, identify the vote that can amend or waive it, whether disproportionate changes need the affected holder's consent, spring-back clauses, and promises made in side letters or negotiations.

## 3. Cap table data — Carta and Linear (January and December 2024)

**Established:** In January 2024 Linear CEO Karri Saarinen said Carta's secondaries team had contacted Linear's angel investors, including an undisclosed family-member investor, about selling Linear shares without the company's involvement. Carta said an employee had violated internal procedures, paused the outreach, and on 2024-01-08 announced it would exit secondary trading. ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/01/08/after-taking-credibility-hit-carta-announces-it-is-exiting-the-secondaries-business-we-have-decided-to-prioritize-trust/), [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2024/01/08/carta-pauses-sales-outreach-after-customer-data-accusation/))

**Unverified:** in December 2024 a founder posting as @ItzSuds said Carta locked his account and blocked cap-table export during a cancellation dispute ([post](https://x.com/ItzSuds/status/1866635451471368441)). This is one user's account; no Carta response was found.

**For the review:** treat the cap-table platform as a processor of sensitive shareholder data; check data-use terms, internal access, bans on soliciting shareholders, and export rights; confirm transfer restrictions and the company's right of first refusal on secondary sales.

## 4. Control through the board — Benchmark and Uber (2016–2018)

**Established:** In June 2016 Uber's board grew from 8 to 11 seats, with Travis Kalanick controlling the three new seats. After Kalanick stepped down as CEO in June 2017, Benchmark (about 13% holder) sued him in Delaware in August 2017 to void those seat rights and remove him from the board. The case was sent to arbitration and dismissed in January 2018 as part of SoftBank's investment and governance changes. ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2017/08/10/benchmark-sues-former-uber-ceo-travis-kalanick/), [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2017/08/30/lawsuit-kalanick-arbitration/), [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2018/01/25/benchmarks-lawsuit-against-former-uber-ceo-kalanick-dismissed/))

**Alleged, never adjudicated:** Benchmark's fraud claims; Kalanick called the suit "completely without merit".

**For the review:** map who can create, fill, and remove each seat today and after future rounds; seat-designation rights become the battleground when trust breaks down.

## 5. Liquidation preferences — Epinions and Shopping.com (2003–2005)

**Established:** Epinions' preferred investors held preferences on the $45M they had invested. In the 2003 merger with DealTime, more than 6 million common shares held by four departed founders, including Naval Ravikant, were valued at zero. Shopping.com, the combined company, went public in October 2004 and was worth about $750M after its first trading day. The founders sued in January 2005; the case reportedly settled on undisclosed terms. ([New York Times, 2005](https://www.nytimes.com/2005/01/26/technology/founders-of-web-site-accuse-backers-of-cheating-them.html), [New York Times, 2004](https://www.nytimes.com/2004/11/01/technology/the-ipo-succeeded-who-was-rewarded.html))

**Alleged, never adjudicated:** that the founders were misled about the company's value and prospects; the defendants called the claims without merit.

**For the review:** model the waterfall below the total preference before agreeing to any sale; a large preference stack can make common worthless in an exit that looks successful later.

## 6. One-sided protection — the Joseph Jacks anecdote (June 2026)

**What exists:** a June 2026 post by Joseph Jacks (OSS Capital) describing an unnamed VC whose term sheets, even for $1M checks, required founders to return 5x, kept full anti-dilution plus additional equity, and allowed firing the founders if the 5x was not reached in three years. ([post](https://x.com/JosephJacks_/status/2067029361325412440))

**Unverified:** the fund, the documents, and how common such terms are. Present it as an illustration, not a market example.

**For the review:** flag return guarantees, performance-based removal rights, and extra equity layered on anti-dilution, and evaluate them together: the combination can remove the investor's downside, keep the upside, and give the investor power to replace the founder.
