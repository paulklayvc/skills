# Deal-term market data

**Data as of: October 2026** (latest editions read: Cooley Q2 2026, Wilson Sonsini Q2 2026, PitchBook-NVCA Q3 2026, Carta Q1–Q2 2026, Osler 2025, Orrick Deal Flow 6.0).

Numbers to cite when saying how common a term is; each number carries its source key and period. When this date is more than six months old, look for newer editions of these reports first (names and cadence in [sources.md](sources.md)) and use this file as the fallback.

How to use the data:
- Law-firm samples (Cooley, WSGR, Osler) reflect their own clients; PitchBook-NVCA covers the broad US market; Carta reflects companies on its platform. Prefer ranges across sources over one precise figure.
- Cooley and WSGR restate the previous quarter in the next report. Cite the latest report and name it.
- Aggressive terms cluster in down, flat, and distressed rounds. Compare a term with data for the same round type when available.
- For UK and European deals, use the Orrick Deal Flow 6.0 data in section 10 and the jurisdiction guidance in [document-map.md](document-map.md); US data is only a rough comparison there.

## Key takeaways for a founder-side reviewer

1. **The standard is a 1x non-participating preference.** Cooley Q2 2026: 95.8% of deals were 1x and 96.4% were non-participating [COOLEY-Q2-26]. WSGR 1H 2026: 97% non-participating [WSGR-Q2-26]. Osler (Canada) 2025: 94.6% were 1x and 3.2% were participating [OSLER-TERMS]. A multiple above 1x or any participation is far outside the norm.
2. **Pari passu is the default; seniority is a minority term.** Senior preferences appeared in 16% of WSGR Series B+ deals in 1H 2026, down from 30% in 2022. In down rounds the share was 46% (2025) [WSGR-Q2-26]. Osler Canada 2025: 20.4% senior [OSLER-TERMS].
3. **Anti-dilution is effectively always broad-based weighted average.** WSGR 1H 2026: 98% broad-based and 1% ratchet [WSGR-Q2-26]. Osler 2025: 100% broad-based [OSLER-TERMS]. Full ratchet or narrow-based is far outside the norm.
4. **Cumulative or accruing dividends are rare.** WSGR 1H 2026: 1% cumulative [WSGR-Q2-26]. Cooley Q2 2026: 3% accruing [COOLEY-Q2-26]. Osler 2025: 4.2% cumulative [OSLER-TERMS].
5. **Redemption rights are uncommon (about 5–7%).** Cooley Q2 2026: 5.4%. WSGR 1H 2026: 5%. Osler 2025: 7.4%.
6. **Pay-to-play is the investor-protective term with real presence.** Cooley Q2 2026: 8.4%, up from 7%. WSGR Series B+: 11% of all rounds in 1H 2026, and 42% of Series B+ down rounds in 2025.
7. **Down rounds run about 11–12% of deals.** Cooley Q2 2026: 12.1%. Carta Q1 2026: 11.4%, its lowest since 2019–2020. WSGR Series B+ Q2 2026: about 3%. Up rounds dominate.
8. **Valuation medians depend heavily on the sample, so use ranges.** Series A median pre-money was $60.3M at PitchBook (2026 YTD) and $80.0M at WSGR (Q2 2026). Seed was $18.3M and $23.0M respectively. AI companies get large premiums.
9. **Typical dilution per round is under 20%.** Carta Q2 2026 benchmarks: seed 19.4%, Series A 18.7%, Series B 13.4% [CARTA-BENCH]. A round taking well over 20% is above median.
10. **SAFEs: post-money with a cap and no discount is the norm.** Carta Q2 2026: 91% post-money. 1H 2026: 73% of post-money SAFEs were cap-only. The median discount is 20% (WSGR, Osler). The overall direction in 2026 is founder-friendly (WSGR, Carta, Fenwick commentary). One exception: Cooley saw small Q2 upticks in pay-to-play, down rounds and accruing dividends.

**Not covered by any source here:** Series A–specific seniority (WSGR reports Series B and later), the distribution of preference multiples (how often 1.5x or 2x appears), US board composition, founder approval of drag-alongs, founder re-vesting, founder personal representations, super pro rata and rights to lead, and US no-shop length. For these, benchmark against the model documents and say so.

## 9. Direction of travel: founder- or investor-friendly?
- **WSGR (Q2 2026):** "negotiating leverage for companies selling preferred stock has moved back toward companies, and investors ... have had less need to insist on the aggressive downside protections that were common in the harder market of 2022 and 2023." Senior preferences fell to 16% (Series B+).
- **Carta (Q1 2026):** "Down rounds are rare again. Dilution is down. Terms favor founders."
- **Fenwick (Q1 2026):** participating preferred, cumulative dividends and >1x preferences "remained rare". Down rounds declined entering 2026.
- **Cooley (Q2 2026):** preference structures "remained favorable to companies". Some quarter-on-quarter upticks in investor-protective items: pay-to-play 7% to 8.4%, down rounds 10.9% to 12.1%, accruing dividends 2.3% to 3%.
- **Osler Canada (2025):** senior preferences fell (25.6% to 20.4%). Redemption rose (4.3% to 7.4%), and series-specific drag thresholds rose (9.5% to 22.9%). Osler links the latter to AI valuation gaps between old and new investors.
- **Market is bifurcated:**
  - PitchBook: AI took 82.7% of 2026 YTD US deal value [NVCA-Q3-26].
  - J.P. Morgan commentary in the same report: conditions are "highly favorable" for perceived category leaders, while for others the process "can feel prolonged and uncertain".
  - WSGR: "a small but stubborn tail of flat and down rounds."
  - Practical consequence: aggressive terms cluster in down, flat and distressed rounds. For example, WSGR 2025 Series B+ down rounds had 46% senior preferences and 42% pay-to-play.
- **SAFEs (WSGR):** median caps fell to $15M in 1H 2026. WSGR notes that post-money caps "often result in a better economic deal for investors".
