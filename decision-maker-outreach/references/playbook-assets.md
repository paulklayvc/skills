## 2. Customer development question bank

**Current state**
- Walk me through the last time you dealt with [problem]. What happened?
- How do you handle it today? What does it cost you (time, money, stress)?
- What have you tried before? Why did you stop?

**Decision & budget**
- If you fixed this, whose budget would it come from?
- Who else would need to agree?
- What would make this a priority this quarter vs. next year?

**Information & trust (this is where entry points come from)**
- Who do you go to for advice on this? A person, a peer group, a consultant?
- Which newsletters, podcasts or accounts do you actually read?
- Are there any chats or communities you're in with peers? Which are genuinely useful?
- Which conferences are worth going to? Which ones are just vendors selling to each other?
- Outside work, what do you do with peers — sports, clubs, dinners?

**Close every call with**
- Who else should I talk to about this? Would you be comfortable introducing me?

Don't pitch. If they ask what you're building, answer in one sentence and turn it back to them.

## 3. Getting the first 10 conversations

- Ask for 20 minutes of *advice*, not a meeting. People say yes to being experts.
- Mine second-degree connections: ask friends, investors, former colleagues "who do you know who is a [role] at a [company type]?"
- Offer something: a summary of what you learn from all interviews, an intro, a relevant resource.
- Every interview ends with a referral ask. Ten conversations usually come from 3–4 seeds.

## 5. Entry-point scoring

For each candidate place, score 1–3 on:
- **Density** — how many ICP-fit people are there?
- **Quality** — decision-makers, or vendors and juniors?
- **Access** — can the user get in naturally and honestly?
- **Intimacy** — can real conversations happen (a padel match beats a 3,000-person expo hall)?

Prioritize high intimacy + decent access, even at lower density.

## 6. "What I do" line + case story

**Line:** "I help [ICP in their words] [get specific outcome] — [short how]."
> "I help iGaming marketing teams keep buying traffic at their old cost now that Meta's bans got aggressive."

**Case story (20–30 seconds):** situation → what we did → concrete result → stop.
> "We just worked with a casino app around 150k users that had three ad accounts killed in a month. We put a layer in front of their Meta setup and they were back to roughly their old CPI within six weeks."

Then say nothing more. Let them ask.

## 7. Cold messages

**Structure (keep under ~80 words, shorter on messengers):**
1. Why *them* specifically — something real (their talk, post, company news, a mutual).
2. The relevant observation or result — one line, no feature list.
3. A low-friction ask — a question, an opinion, a podcast invite. Not "30 min demo?"
4. An easy out.

**Example — value-first, messenger:**
> Hi Anna — saw your talk at [event] on post-iOS attribution, the bit about blended CAC was sharp. I'm recording a short podcast series with iGaming CMOs on what's replacing Meta for acquisition. Would you be up for a 25-min conversation? Totally fine if not.

**Example — case-led, email:**
> Subject: Meta bans at [Company]
>
> Hi Mark — a few iGaming teams your size told me their Meta accounts are getting killed within weeks since the AI optimization rollout. We helped one (~150k users) get back to near their old CPI in six weeks. Is this on your radar at [Company], or have you already solved it? Either answer is useful to me.

**Avoid:** long intros about yourself, attachments, fake familiarity, "just following up" chains, messaging on personal channels the person hasn't made public.

**Follow-up:** one nudge after 5–7 days with a new piece of value (a stat, an article, a relevant intro). Then stop.

## 8. Value-first formats

- Podcast or video interview series with the ICP
- Industry report / benchmark built from interviewing them (they get the results)
- Private community, chat or dinner series for peers
- Expert roundup article featuring their quotes
- Free audit / teardown of something public (their ads, onboarding, pricing page)
- Introductions to people *they* want to meet
